![Nine Orbits, One Point](../assets/images/nine-orbits-one-point.png)

In the book Brave New World by Aldous Huxley, he dated the future from a car. The novel is set in A.F. 632, Anno Fordis, counted from 1908 of the Gregorian calendar, when Ford's Model T went into production. Characters swear "Ford" where we would say "God", and the cross has been trimmed into a T. It's a joke, but also an argument: a civilization organized around mass production will eventually date itself by it.

We are in a similar position, and haven't chosen our year zero yet.

The candidates are easy to list. Turing's 1950 paper<sup><a href="#fn-1" id="ref-1" aria-label="Footnote 1">[1]</a></sup> asked whether machines can think. The 1956 Dartmouth workshop gave the field its name. ChatGPT, in November 2022, is the answer most people will give, because that's when AI became something even our parents used. Each has a flaw. Turing and Dartmouth marked when people started asking, not when the answers started arriving. ChatGPT is a product launch, and is closer to the launch of Model T in the category, but we have a better prospect.

I propose June 2017. When a team at Google published "Attention is All You Need"<sup><a href="#fn-2" id="ref-2" aria-label="Footnote 2">[2]</a></sup> and introduced the transformer. Much of what we now call AI descends from this (directly or indirectly): chatbots, image generators, agents, etc. ChatGPT was the moment the world noticed. The paper was the moment it became possible.

**Call the era A.A., After Attention.**  
**By that count, today is A.A. 9.**

Three reasons I like it,

1. It marks a real discontinuity. Before the transformer, language models existed but scaled badly. Much of what has happend since is the same idea with more compute.

2. The name has a pun built in. Attention is the mechanism inside the model, and attention is also what the economy around it harvests. A calendar that quietly links the two is doing the kind of work Huxley did.

3. It come with a liturgy. The paper's title is already a slogan, and "for Attention's sake" works as a mild curse the way "for Ford's sake" does in the novel.

Now the objections. A.A. also stands for Alcoholics Anonymous, which I consider a feature. Nobody promised the future would be tidy.

Someone will point out that attention mechnisms predate 2017. Bahdanau and colleagues used them in machine translation in 2014.<sup><a href="#fn-3" id="ref-3" aria-label="Footnote 3">[3]</a></sup> That's true, but their version was bolted onto recurrent networks. The 2017 paper made attention the architecture, and that's the step the rest of the field followed.

Some might say that any anchor is arbitrary. Of course, A.F. was arbitrary too.  
A calendar (anchor) doesn't have to be correct, only shared and it should make you notice what a society is built around.

The last point in why I'd like this to cach on, even as a running joke. Dating our era from a paper instead of a product or a person says something true: the thing that changed the world was an idea, written down, published openly and then scaled by everyone with a data center (compute).

**So A.A. 9. The year of our Attention.**  
**Nine years in, and its getting more wierder and exciting than we all thought.**
